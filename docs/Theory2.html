1 Theory

1.1 Class-Based Segregation and Homophily

US society is strongly segregated based on class, in terms of both income and educational attainment (Mijs and Roe 2021). Analysis of General Social Survey data indicates that an increasing difference in the number of years of education received negatively affected the probability of naming someone as a core confidant, and that this association has even become somewhat stronger between 1985 and 2004 (Smith, McPherson, and Smith-Lovin 2014). This is likely to extend to college communities. Studies on the experiences of first-generation college students highlight that classism (Allan, Garriott, and Keene 2016), microaggressions (Gray et al. 2017; ÁlvarezRivadulla et al. 2023), and insensitivity (Salusky et al. 2022) create barriers to forming and maintaining cross-class friendships. On a micro level, this suggests the following hypothesis:

H1: Students prefer maintaining friendships with peers who have the same parental educational background.

On a macro level, homophily suggests that the college social network will be segregated along students’ parental educational backgrounds:

H2: Students who hang out together are more similar with regards to parental educational backgrounds than students who do not hang out together.

1.2 Family Socialisation

Importantly however, social distance between different group categories is not fixed. As evidenced by the observed tendency for homophily on specific categories to fluctuate over time (Smith, McPherson, and Smith-Lovin 2014), it is a context-dependent measure. Just as context may change over time on a national level, it may differ between communities or personal networks. This study therefore considers class categorisation to be a social construct, and its relevance for behaviour as something that is socially learnt.

An important share of social learning happens within families.

Cross-class embeddedness!

H3: Students whose parents have different educational backgrounds (“mixed background”) will demonstrate lower levels of homophily in friendship maintenance than students whose parents have the attained the same level of education.
